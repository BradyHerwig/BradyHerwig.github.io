Case study

NamUs Missing Persons Analysis

How do demographic and geographic characteristics of NamUs missing-person listings differ between juveniles and adults—and which factors best distinguish the two groups when modeled with logistic regression versus a decision tree?

Classification EDA Scikit-learn Public data

Slide 1 of 4

What it shows

End-to-end analysis of public NamUs missing-person listings: clean the extract, explore juvenile vs adult profiles (age, sex, race/ethnicity, geography, year), then classify age_group from sex, state, and race/ethnicity only—never age, which would leak the label. On the test set, logistic regression led on overall accuracy, while the decision tree recovered more juveniles (higher juvenile recall and F1). Results describe patterns in a listing database, not the true risk of going missing.

What I learned

Accuracy alone can hide minority-class failure—here adults dominate the data, so a majority baseline looks strong while juveniles are under-recalled. Comparing an additive logistic model to a tree makes the cost of ignoring interactions concrete. I also practiced leakage-aware feature design, stratified evaluation, and careful framing of sensitive public data so findings stay about listings, not people at risk.
